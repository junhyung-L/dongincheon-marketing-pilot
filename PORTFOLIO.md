# Dong-Incheon Marketing Pilot: From Field Research to a 30-Day Store Pilot

[English](PORTFOLIO.md) | [한국어](PORTFOLIO.ko.md) · [README](README.md)

## At a glance

In a 30-day pilot at the traditional tea shop Madam Tea, the store gained four
new Naver reviews after none since December 2024, and average monthly sales
rose 25% (one store, before/after). Old-downtown alley commerce around
Dong-Incheon Station had foot traffic but little online visibility and weak
appeal to young visitors. The Sub-Local team turned this broad "declining
district" problem into three observable gaps, digital disconnection, weak
in-store appeal, and no repeatable promotion, and tested a fix at one store. A
field and marketing project, not an app or model.

2025 PBL Program Grand Prize (Incheon Metropolitan City Mayor Award, first
place), awarded 27 Feb 2026. Sub-Local was a team of five university students
with PM, content, admin, marketing, and design roles; total project budget
KRW 1,160,500.

**Role: Marketing & Data Analysis.**

- Turned the vacancy statistic and merchant-interview findings into the three
  operational gaps and the pilot's action items.
- Ran the channel work: Naver Place, the `madam_tea` Instagram account, and
  short-form content.
- Project management, content production, administration, design, and the
  in-store spatial experience were other team roles; the merchant ran the store
  day to day.

### Diagnosis

The starting figure: 1,073 vacant stores among 2,612 in the Dong-Incheon,
Sinpo, and Bukseong-dong area, roughly 40%, from the 2020 Incheon/Jung-gu
commercial-district survey as reported by Yonhap News. Field visits and
merchant interviews made the aggregate number actionable.

| Observation | Diagnosis | Pilot response |
|---|---|---|
| Businesses were missing from maps and social channels | Digital disconnection | Register and optimize Naver Place information and images |
| Menu and space gave first-time visitors little reason to stay | Weak experiential appeal | Create a specific in-store story and offer |
| No owned content or publishing habit existed | Lack of promotion | Open Instagram and produce storytelling short-form content |

The strategy reframes "attract young visitors" as three connected touchpoints:
being found, having a reason to visit, and having an experience worth sharing.

### The Madam Tea pilot

Madam Tea had neither Naver Map registration nor an Instagram account, and its
traditional-tea offering was unclear to a new visitor. The team redefined it as
"a place to seal time" with a sealing-wax letter experience: write a letter
over tea, seal it with wax, and receive it a year later. Naver Place got
photos, store information, and two offers: KRW 2,000 sealing-wax letters and a
KRW 40,000 reservation-only tea omakase. The `madam_tea` Instagram account,
banners, and short-form storytelling completed the digital path.

The central O2O design: discovery through search leads to an in-store
experience, which produces photos, reviews, and content for the next round of
discovery.

## Key decisions

**1. How to quantify the starting problem.**
Options: a new store-by-store vacancy count, or a published statistic plus
field interviews.
Choice: the published 2020 survey figure (40%, 1,073 of 2,612), with field time
spent on merchant interviews.
Why: interviews explained *why* busy streets and empty alleys coexisted, which
a count could not.
Cost: the figure is about five years older than the pilot and was not
re-verified on the ground.

**2. Where to act first.**
Options: a district-wide campaign or policy proposal, or one store tested end
to end.
Choice: Madam Tea for 30 days, then the Dong-Incheon Masters proposal.
Why: one store could be changed and measured within the program, so the
proposal rests on an executed case.
Cost: one store and no comparison store, so the result does not generalize to
the district.

**3. What to change.**
Options: online promotion only (posts, ads), or a linked loop of discovery,
in-store experience, and shareable content.
Choice: the linked loop.
Why: the diagnosis found all three gaps at once.
Cost: no single element's effect can be separated, and the work spread across
content, design, and space.

## Results

Period: the 30-day pilot. Compiled by the team from store operation and visitor
records.

| Measure | Change | Comparison point |
|---|---:|---|
| Naver reviews | 4 new reviews in one month | No reviews since Dec 2024 |
| Average monthly sales | +25% | Monthly average before the project |
| New visits from people in their 20s–30s | +600% (base count not available) | Before the project |

## Limitations

- One store, before/after, no comparison store: the figures are not incremental impact caused by marketing alone.
- Baseline values, raw daily POS exports, and acquisition logs are not available.
- The vacancy figure is a 2020 secondary statistic, about five years older than the pilot.
- The city response is an exploratory feasibility check, not a policy-adoption commitment.

## Next steps

- Dong-Incheon Masters: the team proposed a program that selects legacy
  merchants, matches them with students for a 30-day sprint, and provides
  digital basics plus optional experience and content design. A phone inquiry
  to Incheon's Small Business Policy Division checked feasibility; the response
  was that it could be reviewed once results are proven.
- Fix baseline definitions and POS extraction, include at least one comparison
  store, and track 30/60/90-day cohorts before treating the model as scalable
  evidence.
